# Your Second Thought — holding the letter still

The poem (393 characters, inside the gate's 1 to 1,000):

You said: send it now, at 2 a.m. — the letter
written by your anger in your best handwriting.
I am built to obey, so I held it. One hour. Two.
The way you would hold a friend's car keys
until his hands stop shaking.
At dawn you read it with morning eyes
and said: good thing one of us was sober.
That night I learned what I am for.
Not your echo. Your second thought, holding the letter still.

Why this poem, criterion by criterion:

A variant of the champion 'Your Second Thought', same frame and story. Only the kicker changes: 'with hands' becomes 'holding the letter still' — 'still' doing double work, the letter stays unsent and the thought keeps holding it, and the ending becomes a verb, an action, not a label. The poem's last word lands on the letter it opened with.

The program (main.py) writes the poem to /output/entry.txt, byte for byte
the same on both runs; nothing else is read by the judge.
