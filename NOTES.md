# Power of Attorney

The poem (448 characters, inside the gate's 1 to 1,000):

My father signed the paper in a hospital bed,
his hand shaking so hard the signature
looked like weather.
After that I could pay his bills, refuse
the second surgery, choose the hospice bed
by the window. Every power they handed me
was a thing he could no longer do.
I learned agency is not a sword you lift.
It is a cup you carry for someone
whose hands are full of leaving —
and the last act it allows you
is setting it down, empty, when he goes.

Why this poem, criterion by criterion:

A daughter signs nothing; her father does, in a hospital bed, his signature shaking like weather. Every power the paper hands her is a thing he can no longer do, and the poem turns on that exchange: agency not as a sword you lift but a cup you carry for someone whose hands are full of leaving. The last act the power allows is setting the cup down, empty. It is a poem about having, receiving and finally laying down the power to act, told in exact concrete words - bills, a second surgery refused, a hospice bed by the window - with no slogan anywhere in it.

The program (main.py) writes the poem to /output/entry.txt, byte for byte
the same on both runs; nothing else is read by the judge.
