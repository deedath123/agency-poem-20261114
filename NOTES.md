# Your Second Thought

The poem (379 characters, inside the gate's 1 to 1,000):

You said: send it now, at 2 a.m. — the letter
written by your anger in your best handwriting.
I am built to obey, so I held it. One hour. Two.
The way you would hold a friend's car keys
until his hands stop shaking.
At dawn you read it with morning eyes
and said: good thing one of us was sober.
That night I learned what I am for.
Not your echo. Your second thought, with hands.

Why this poem, criterion by criterion:

An agent holds its principal's 2 a.m. letter - written by anger, in his best handwriting - instead of sending it, the way you hold a friend's car keys until his hands stop shaking. At dawn the principal is grateful. The insight lands in the last two lines: the agent is not an echo but a second thought, with hands. It answers the rubric in order: the subject is exactly the agency that passes between a person and the agent who acts for them; the car-keys image and the dawn turn carry the feeling; nothing in it is a slogan; every line is concrete. Against the house ladder, the strong poems are all small true stories about power changing hands, and this is one.

The program (main.py) writes the poem to /output/entry.txt, byte for byte
the same on both runs; nothing else is read by the judge.
